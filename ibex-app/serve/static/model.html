About the model

How a score is produced, and what it can honestly claim

Ibex turns a consented bank-transaction history into a probability of default, then into a score, then into a tamper-evident record. This page documents that path end to end: where the data comes from, the exact features used, the mechanics of the score, how the result is proved, how the error bars are calculated, where bias could enter, and what we would fix next. Every figure is reproducible from the artifacts in the repository.

1 · Where the data comes from

Step 1Applicant picks their bank and is redirected to it through TrueLayer.
Step 2They authenticate with the bank directly. Ibex never sees the credentials.
Step 3The bank returns a one-time authorisation code, exchanged for a short-lived access token.
Step 4Ibex pulls accounts, balances, up to 24 months of transactions, and the account-holder record.
Step 5Transactions are normalised into dated, signed entries with a counterparty label.
How the data is attached to a person

The name on the score is not typed in by the applicant. It comes from the account-holder record the bank itself returns, which the bank has already verified under its own KYC obligations.

That is what makes the score meaningful: an applicant cannot present someone else. The identity, the transaction history and the resulting score all originate from the same authenticated bank session.

Consent, and its limits

Access is scoped to the specific permissions granted -- account details, balances, transactions and identity -- and expires. It is revocable at the bank at any time, without involving Ibex.

The same normalisation code path serves both the live bank feed and the training data, so a feature computed in production is computed exactly as it was in training.

2 · The pipeline, end to end

  1. Data intake

    1,526,660 applications, each with a 24-month repayment history. The live open-banking feed enters the same code path as the training data, so nothing is computed one way in research and another way in production.

  2. Feature construction

    A recurring-payment detector groups debits by counterparty and rounded amount, then keeps only clusters that behave like obligations: a minimum number of payments, a median gap of 5 to 40 days, and an amount coefficient of variation at or below 0.35. Surviving streams are classified as loan, card or BNPL. From those, and from the declared fields, 74 candidate features are built.

  3. Parity verification

    Every feature from the open-banking path is checked against the reference implementation value for value, at a tolerance of 1e-6 absolute plus 1e-4 relative, with both-missing counted as agreement. Mean match rate is 0.93. Anything that cannot be reproduced from a bank feed is refused, whatever its predictive power.

  4. Feature selection

    Four gates in order: null-importance screening against 30 label-shuffled runs at the 95th percentile, a correlation prune at 0.90, an information-value floor of 0.005, then permutation importance on validation. 74 candidates reduce to 16.

  5. Training

    Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM), 132 rounds, native missing-value handling, monotone constraints where the economic direction is unambiguous. Chosen over XGBoost and a blend on calibration-set Gini, never on the evaluation set.

  6. Calibration

    Raw model output is not a probability. Isotonic regression on a held-out 305,332-row slice maps rank to calibrated risk. Brier falls from 0.1789 to 0.0263; expected calibration error is 0.0057.

  7. Scoring and proof

    The calibrated probability becomes a score, the floor and ceiling are applied, reason codes are attached, and the score event is hashed and anchored on chain.

Why gradient-boosted trees

Credit features are tabular, monotone in known directions, and full of genuinely missing values. Boosted trees handle all three natively: no imputation pipeline that could drift between training and serving, and monotone constraints are enforced at the split level rather than hoped for. A neural network was never a serious candidate -- with 16 engineered features there is no representation to learn, and the opacity cost is real.

How LightGBM was chosen

LightGBM, XGBoost and a blend were trained on identical features and identical splits, and compared on the calibration slice only. The evaluation slice was never touched during selection, so the comparison cannot leak into the reported figures. LightGBM won on calibration-set Gini; the blend added a second model's explanation and monitoring burden without a measured gain, so it was rejected on parsimony.

How the shape is fixed

Training stopped at 132 rounds, the best iteration on validation rather than a fixed budget. Monotone constraints apply wherever the economic direction is unambiguous -- more arrears can never raise a score, a longer clean streak can never lower one -- so the model cannot learn a perverse relationship even where the data is thin enough to suggest one by chance.

Learning from rare defaults

Defaults are rare -- about 4% of rows -- so a model that called every loan clean would still be about 96% accurate, and the training has to be forced to care. Correction one happens during training: LightGBM's scale_pos_weight is set to the ratio of clean to defaulted rows (about 25 to 1), so each default contributes as much to the loss as roughly 25 clean loans, and splits that isolate defaulters become worth making.

The side effect is that the raw margins now describe a synthetic world where defaults are roughly half the population, so they are no longer real probabilities. Correction two happens after training: isotonic regression, fit with PAVA on the held-out calibration slice, maps raw margins back onto real probabilities -- a monotone step function over 2,000 quantile bins, with a Beta prior of strength 100 centred on the calibration base rate (3.88%) pulling thin bins back toward it, and the hybrid tail plus the 0.92% floor handling the sparse extreme. The weight fixes ranking power; the isotonic restores probability meaning.

The top feature carries the largest share of importance by a wide margin, which is itself listed as a model risk below.

Obligations before transactions

Raw transactions are noise; obligations are the causal substrate of default. The detector groups debits by counterparty and rounded amount, then keeps only streams that behave like commitments: enough payments to be a pattern, a median gap of 5 to 40 days (weekly to monthly cadence), and an amount coefficient of variation at or below 0.35 so irregular transfers cannot pass as loans. Everything the model knows about repayment behaviour is computed on those streams, never on raw spend.

Persistence is the signal

The design favours sustained behaviour over single events. The current clean streak (0.158) is the most important feature by a wide margin, the longest historical streak (0.015) ranks alongside the arrears counts, and the autocorrelation lags exist because one late month predicting the next is more informative than either month alone. A 24-month window balances recency against having enough history for a streak to mean anything.

Declared data, handled honestly

Declared categoricals are encoded as a stable MD5-derived code -- the same string maps to the same number in training and in production, which is what makes train/serve parity checkable at all. Declared income is cross-checked against detected income through declared_income_gap, so an inflated claim is itself evidence. Housing was engineered twice (a homeowner flag and a tenure code) and both candidates were rejected at selection, which is why the form no longer asks it; education was removed by policy, not by a gate.

Missing is modelled, not filled in

Four of the sixteen features describe the data rather than the applicant: whether income was detected, whether the file is thin, whether anything was declared, and how many inputs are missing. LightGBM routes missing values natively, so a thin file takes a different path through the same trees instead of being silently imputed to the mean. Absence of history is treated as absence of evidence, not evidence of risk.

4 · The mechanics

score = 427.1229 + 57.7078 × ln( (1 − PD) / PD )

The multiplier is 40 / ln(2), so every doubling of the odds of repayment adds exactly 40 points. The offset anchors a score of 600 to odds of 20:1. The gap between two scores is therefore a statement about odds, not an arbitrary ranking.

The probability floor

The model may not claim a risk below 0.916%. That is not a preference, it is where the evidence runs out: in the safest bucket only a few hundred applicants with a handful of defaults support the estimate, so a Beta posterior over that bucket sets the lowest defensible claim.

The floor is fixed policy; its value is re-derived at every retrain.

The score ceiling

Because the floor caps how low PD can go, it caps the score at 697.4. About 0.17% of applicants reach it, so it is a genuine limit on claims rather than a bunching artefact.

A consequence worth stating plainly: band A cannot currently be reached. Awarding a top band would require evidence this data does not contain.

Bands

A from 720, B from 660, C from 600, D from 540, E below. Boundaries are fixed on the odds scale and are not rebased at retrain, so a B this year means the same odds as a B last year.

5 · Proof: hashing and the smart contract

The chain is used for one job only: proving that a score existed, in a specific form, at a specific time, from a specific model. No personal data, no transactions and no score value are written to it.

How a lender verifies

The applicant hands over their score-event file. The lender pastes it into the verification page; the server recomputes the hash and compares it against the value anchored on chain. A match means the file is byte-for-byte what was issued.

Verification is pull-based and consent-first: the lender can only check a record the applicant has chosen to share.

Why hashes rather than data

A hash is one-way. Publishing it proves a document existed without revealing anything inside it, so the chain can be public while the financial data never leaves the private server.

The chain adds no predictive power. It is an integrity mechanism, not part of the model.

7 · Is it fair?

Every decision is explained

Each score carries the specific features that pushed it down and those that held it up, with their contributions, for that applicant rather than an average.

Declared fields are optional

Employment, income type and housing can be left blank without penalty. Whether the applicant declared at all is carried as its own flag, so a blank is never silently read as a negative.

Decision support, not a verdict

Ibex returns a probability and a band. It declines no one. The lending decision, and the policy behind it, stays with the lender.

Honest limits

The service publishes its own ceiling and its own error bars next to every score. A model that cannot say how uncertain it is should not be trusted with the decision.

8 · Stability, and how the error bars are calculated

Measured across 32 origination weeks

The observed default rate drifts from 4.49% to 2.27% across the evaluation window. It does not glide down: most of the step occurs at week 65, where weekly volume simultaneously falls from a pre-break peak of 17,164 to a trough of 825 applications before recovering, so this is better described as a regime break than a smooth drift. The 5 weeks before the break carry 23.3% of the evaluation rows but 38.5% of the defaults. Right-censoring contributes at the very end, since the newest cohorts have had least time to default, but neither effect is the model decaying: the booster is held fixed across the whole window.

Design effect 2.20×

Applications arrive in weekly cohorts sharing an economy and a credit policy, so they are not independent. Resampling whole weeks inflates variance by 4.86×, making the honest standard error 2.20× the naive one. 305,332 evaluation rows carry the information of roughly 62,833 independent ones.

The stability figure

Per-vintage Gini is computed for every week with enough defaults to support it. A stable model gives a flat series with small spread; a drifting one shows trend. It is recomputed at each retrain and reported with the model, not asserted once. On the current build it is 0.3766: mean weekly Gini 0.4018, slope +0.00347 per week, so the deterioration penalty is zero and the whole gap is week-to-week scatter.

Every interval printed beside a score comes from the same six-step procedure. The important choice is the second one: what gets resampled.

  1. Divide the evaluation set into blocks

    The 305,332 evaluation rows fall into 32 weekly origination cohorts. A cohort shares a macroeconomic environment, a marketing mix and a credit policy, so two applicants from the same week are more alike than two drawn at random.

  2. Resample whole weeks, not individual rows

    Draw 32 blocks with replacement to build a synthetic evaluation set of the same size in expectation. Cohort sizes are unequal, from 825 to 17,886 applications, so the row count of a replicate varies by about 9% around 305,332. Resampling rows would silently assume every application is independent, which is the assumption that fails here. This single choice is what separates the honest standard error from the flattering one.

  3. Refit the calibrator on every replicate

    The isotonic calibration is re-estimated from scratch inside each replicate. Reusing one calibrator would treat the calibration step as error-free and understate the uncertainty on every PD.

  4. Recompute every published quantity

    On each replicate: Gini, the PD at scores 600, 660 and 697, the probability floor, and the tail counts below the floor knot. Nothing is derived analytically from another quantity.

  5. Repeat 500 times, then read percentiles

    The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the replicate distribution give the interval. Not the point estimate plus or minus 1.96 standard errors: the Gini distribution is skewed by +0.25 and the floor distribution by +1.30, so a symmetric interval would put both ends in the wrong place.

  6. Map PD bounds through the score formula

    The score interval is the PD interval pushed through score = 427.1229 + 57.7078 ln((1 − PD)/PD). Because that transform is non-linear, the resulting score interval is asymmetric around the point score -- correctly so.

How many times things actually run

Selection is not a single pass. The null-importance gate shuffles the labels 30 times and keeps only features whose real importance beats the 95th percentile of those null runs. The permutation-importance gate then permutes each surviving feature 3 times on the validation slice and averages the Gini drop. Inference runs 500 whole-week bootstrap replicates. The bootstrap never changes anyone's score -- it exists only to measure uncertainty; scoring itself is always a single point estimate on the applicant's real data.

Why a bootstrap at all

There is no closed-form standard error for a Gini once two things hold at once: applicants arrive in correlated weekly cohorts, and the calibrator is itself estimated from data. DeLong's formula assumes independence and a fixed calibration, so it can only understate. Resampling whole weeks and refitting the calibrator inside every replicate is the only way to carry both sources of uncertainty into the interval.

How the interval is read off

Each replicate yields one value of every published quantity: Gini, the PD at each reference score, the floor. The reported interval is the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of that distribution of 500 -- not the point estimate plus or minus 1.96 standard errors. The replicate distributions are skewed (+0.25 for Gini, +1.30 for the floor), so a symmetric interval would misplace both ends; the percentile interval assumes no shape at all.

9 · Limitations and what comes next

Known limitations
  • Declared fields cannot be verified from a bank feed alone, so they are usable for pricing but not for underwriting on their own.
  • One feature carries the largest share of measured importance by a wide margin; concentration that high is a model risk in itself.
  • Training labels come only from accepted applicants, so the model has never observed how rejected ones would have behaved.
  • Balance trajectories and income-regime shifts are not available in the current feed, capping how much behavioural signal can be extracted.
Planned work
  • Hyperparameter search, expected to add roughly 0.005 to 0.015 Gini on the current feature set.
  • A point-in-time audit of the dominant feature, to confirm no information from after the decision date leaks into it.
  • Promote per-vintage stability from a reported diagnostic to a term in model selection, so a slightly weaker but steadier model can win.
  • Reject inference, to correct the acceptance bias in the training labels.
  • Population stability monitoring in production, alerting when live feature distributions drift from training.
  • Disparate-impact testing as soon as a dataset with protected labels can lawfully be used for it.
