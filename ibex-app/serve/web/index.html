FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Do I need a UK credit history?

No. That is the point of the service. The score is built from how you have actually handled money over the last 24 months in your bank account, not from a credit file you may not have.

If you have recently moved to the UK and no lender can see you, this gives you something to show them.

Will this affect my credit file?

No. Nothing is recorded with a credit reference agency, no search is left on your file, and Ibex is not itself a credit reference agency. Running a score here is invisible to any other lender unless you choose to share the result.

What exactly can you see in my bank account?

Account details, balances, up to 24 months of transactions, and the account-holder record your bank holds for you. Access is read-only: no payment can be initiated, and nothing can be moved.

Do you store my banking login?

No, and we never see it. You authenticate on your own bank’s website. The bank returns a scoped, expiring token to us, which grants exactly the permissions you approved and nothing else.

Can I withdraw access later?

Yes, at any time, from your bank’s own app or website, without asking us. Open-banking consent is time-limited by design and lapses on its own if it is not renewed.

Why is my score capped? Why can nobody get band A?

The model refuses to claim a risk lower than 0.92%, because below that level the data contains too few comparable applicants to support the claim. That floor caps the score at 697.4.

Band A starts at 720, so it is currently unreachable. That is deliberate. Awarding a top band would mean asserting a level of certainty the evidence does not carry.

Why does declared information matter if you cannot verify it?

It adds context the transactions alone do not carry, such as whether income is salaried or self-employed. It is genuinely predictive, which is why it is in the model.

It is also optional, and leaving it blank is recorded as its own fact rather than treated as a negative. Because it cannot be verified from a bank feed, it is suitable for pricing but should not carry an underwriting decision on its own.

What is actually stored on the blockchain?

Only hashes. Your name, your transactions and even your score are never written to the chain. A hash proves a specific record existed at a specific time without revealing anything inside it.

It means a lender can confirm a score has not been edited, while your financial data stays in the encrypted database.

Is this a lending decision?

No. Ibex returns a probability and a band and declines no one. What a lender does with that, and the policy behind it, remains entirely theirs.

How accurate is it, honestly?

A Gini of 0.3835, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.3613 to 0.4146. That is respectable for a model built without any credit-bureau data, and well short of what a major lender achieves with a full credit file.

Every score is published with its own interval rather than a single number, because a model that cannot state its own uncertainty should not be trusted with the decision.

What if I have barely any transaction history?

A thin-file flag is set and carried through the model explicitly, so a short history is treated as missing evidence rather than as evidence of risk. You will still get a score, with a wider interval around it.

On-chain proof, off-chain privacy

Your bank authenticates you directly; transactions are read-only and processed inside the scoring session. Only a salted cryptographic hash of each score event is anchored to Polygon — no transactions, no name, no PII on-chain.
