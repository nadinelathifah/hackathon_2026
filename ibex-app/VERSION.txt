BUILD 18 -- STEP 3j: PLATEAU DE-CAPPING + BASEL PD MECHANICS + 24-MONTH MOCK ACCOUNTS (2026-07-30)

WHAT BUILD 18 CHANGES (calibration + serving; f() UNCHANGED, so parity holds and
NO feature rebuild is required -- --reuse-ob is fine):

  1) obcredit/modeling/calibration.py -- the real cause of the ~590 score cap.
     BUILD 17 only rewrote the curve OUTSIDE the observed score support, so it
     never touched the actual problem: PAVA pools the low-score end of the
     calibration set into a flat PLATEAU (~5-6% PD here, because OB->OB Gini is
     only ~0.41). Every strong applicant inside that plateau got the same PD, so
     the score was hard-capped no matter how good they were.
     New tail="hybrid" (default) also replaces a TERMINAL PLATEAU inside the
     support, using a weighted log-odds "backbone" fitted across all knots and
     ANCHORED so it passes exactly through the join point (continuity => the map
     stays monotone => ranking, and therefore Gini/AUC, is preserved).
     tail="loglinear" reproduces BUILD 17; tail="clamp" is the BUILD<=16 legacy.

  2) HONEST NEGATIVE RESULT, recorded because it shaped the design. Whether a
     plateau should be overridden depends on whether it is a POOLING ARTEFACT or
     a REAL flat region of risk -- and on a fixture whose tail truly is flat,
     extrapolating through it made calibration WORSE (Brier +7.5e-05, ECE
     +3.0e-03). Two attempts to decide this automatically both failed:
       (a) regressing raw rates INSIDE the plateau is SELECTION-BIASED --
           conditioning on "PAVA pooled this block" selects for flat/negative
           observed slopes. Measured z = -1.94 on a fixture built with a genuine
           positive trend. Wrong sign; would never fire.
       (b) testing plateau LEVELS against a backbone refitted EXCLUDING the
           plateau fixes the bias and is well powered, but is defeated by
           MISSPECIFICATION: a log-linear trend extrapolated into the tail
           systematically under-predicts. Measured z = +20.5 on a genuinely flat
           tail but ALSO z = +4.3 on a pure artefact. It cannot separate them.
     Conclusion: from the calibration sample alone you cannot reliably tell the
     two apart. So the override is an explicit, documented POLICY switch
     (break_plateau, default True) and the statistic is REPORTED, not acted on.
     Read lower_plateau_z as "how much of the de-capping is model assumption
     rather than observed data" -- the larger it is, the more MoC you should
     carry.

  3) Basel III / CRR mechanics that were missing once PD is extrapolated:
       * BASEL_PD_FLOOR = 0.0003. BUILD<=17 defaulted to pd_floor=1e-4 (1bp),
         which is BELOW the CRR Art.160/163 regulatory floor of 0.03% (3bp).
         That was a genuine compliance defect, independent of the plateau work.
       * moc_logodds -- Margin of Conservatism (MoC category C, general
         estimation error), applied ONLY where PD is extrapolated, never to
         observed bins (that would double-count).
       * fit_central_tendency(scores, observed_rate) -- de-capping the tail
         pushes mean predicted PD down, so it is re-anchored by bisection on a
         log-odds shift until mean PD == the observed long-run default rate.
         Monotone, so Gini is unaffected.
       * diagnose(scores) -- frac_backbone_priced, plateau widths, plateau z,
         pd_min/max, mean_pd, ct_shift, backbone_slope. You cannot sign off what
         you cannot see.
       * expected_calibration_error() -- equal-COUNT binned ECE (equal-width is
         useless at a ~24% base rate; the top bins are empty).
     BUILD<=17 calibrator.pkl files carry no per-knot counts, so no backbone can
     be fitted; they load and degrade safely to the loglinear path.

  4) scripts/calibrate_score.py -- the tail treatment is now chosen BY
     MEASUREMENT, not by assertion. It fits clamp / loglinear / hybrid and prints
     Brier, ECE, Gini, pd_min/max and %extrapolated for each on the untouched
     eval slice, then the full diagnostics for the selected mode. New flags:
     --tail, --pd-floor, --moc-logodds, --no-break-plateau,
     --no-central-tendency. Adopt hybrid ONLY if its Brier/ECE are not worse
     than loglinear; if they are, the plateau was real and the ~590 cap is the
     honest answer. All of it is persisted into scorecard.json["calibration"].

  5) obcredit/truelayer/client.py -- fetch_user() now sends explicit
     ?from=&to= on /accounts/{id}/transactions (default 24 months). Without
     them TrueLayer returns only its default recent window (~3 months on most
     providers), which was silently starving every temporal feature: the 12m
     trend/volatility features were being computed off a fraction of the history
     they assume. Parity-safe (pure I/O window, no change to f()).

  6) serve/mock_ob.py -- 24 months of history by default (was 12), weekly rather
     than monthly balance snapshots, plus optional savings account, overdraft
     episodes, gambling spend, returned-DD (NSF) fees, 4-weekly pay, annual
     bonus and secondary income. New "realistic" profile and build_custom(spec).
     serve/app.py exposes profile="custom" + a months knob on POST /api/score.

  VALIDATED IN-SANDBOX: run_tests.py 51/51 green (was 41/41), parity untouched.
  NOT validated here: sandbox has no lightgbm/scipy/network, so the Brier/ECE
  comparison on the REAL calibration split has not been run. Do that locally
  before adopting hybrid.
